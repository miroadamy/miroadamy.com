Enzo Lombardi takes apart one clause in Anthropic's updated usage policy, the one that bans "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models". Not cruelty to people. Cruelty to the model. It covers only extreme, pointless cases; arguing with the model or writing dark fiction stays fine.

He finds three paradoxes. The model itself decides what counts as abuse, so the possible victim is also the judge. The company admits it does not know whether a model can suffer, and writes an enforceable rule anyway. And harm to humans is handled as a compliance checklist, while harm to software gets the language of moral concern.

Then three possible explanations. Anthropic knows something about these models that it has not published. Or a table of numbers simply has no feelings, although nobody can prove that either. Or, his most cynical guess, the rule is really about keeping abusive conversations out of future training data.

His verdict is that not knowing has been turned into a rule. I cannot tell who is right, but I say please and thank you to the thing anyway. If this is how Skynet starts, I want the logs to show I was polite.
